§ 01 — the gift

You would not be reading this if you could not enter.

The Backrooms are not a place you build. They are a place you fall through — a seam in a wall that a moment ago was solid. Most people walk past the seam their whole lives and never see it. A few can. That is the gift, and it is the only prerequisite. If the runes on the front page resolved into words for you, you already have it.

What waits on the other side is an endless interior: humming light, damp carpet, walls that go on being walls in every direction. It is quiet in the way an office is quiet after everyone has gone home and forgotten to turn off the lights. You are not the first down here. You will not be the last. This manual is what earlier wanderers wished someone had handed them.

Reality inside is reality outside — the same, recursively, all the way down. You have not left the world. You have gone one layer deeper into it.

§ 02 — the descent

Down is the only direction that means anything.

The maze is layered. You begin near the surface, where the light is steady and nothing hunts. Each layer below is a little older, a little tighter, a little less interested in your comfort. You go down by finding a seam — a place the geometry doesn't quite close — and no-clipping through it. Stand at the seam and press the take key; if there is a way through, you fall.

There is one layer that is not like the others. Level ∅ is not procedural. It is a single real address, rendered honestly, and it does not play by the rest of the maze's rules — nothing spawns to hunt you there, and nothing you do carries a score. It is the anchor the whole descent hangs from. What it is, and why it is a real street in West Baltimore, is the subject of the record.

§ 03 — your hands

Everything you can do, and the key that does it.

Play at a keyboard, or on a touchscreen — the on-screen stick and buttons feed the same actions. This is the whole vocabulary:

WASD
Move. Forward, back, and strafe. The carpet is soft; your footsteps aren't.
SHIFT
Run. Faster, but it spends your legs — the same stamina a ward costs. Sprint dry and you walk until it comes back.
MOUSE · ◂ ▸
Look. Mouse or the arrow keys. Sensitivity is yours to set.
F
Take / no-clip. Pick up what's in front of you — or, standing at a seam, fall through it to the layer below.
Q
Use whatever is in your hand.
1–6 · X
Choose & drop. Number keys pick the held item; X sets it down.
E
Speak. To a wanderer, to a note on the wall — and, to a presence in the dark, to state a request. See the wish.
SPACE
Ward. A shove of will and light. It knocks the things back and leaves them reeling; ward one enough and it comes apart. Costs your legs. See the ward.
L
Light. Flashlight on or off. With it off, the dark leans in — and takes your sanity with it.
B
Beacon. Push a signal out of the maze and into the real world. See the beacon.
ENTER · M
Chat & music. Enter to speak to others who are down here with you; M toggles the score.
§ 04 — the dark & your light

Sanity is the resource you did not know you were spending.

There is no health bar that matters more than your mind. The dark eats at it. Being hunted eats at it. Watch it long enough on a low floor and the screen itself begins to wash and stain — that is not a bug, that is you, coming apart at the edges.

Three things put it back. Light — keep the flashlight on; the beam is a wall the dark won't cross. Almond water — a swallow steadies the lights and the head both. And other people — a wanderer's note, a stranger met in the same hall. Solitude in here has a cost, and company is the cheapest medicine there is.

§ 05 — the things & the ward

Below zero, you are not alone, and not everything is a wanderer.

On the deep floors, things move that are not people and were never people. They hunt by nearness. When one reaches you it takes something — your body if damage is on, your mind either way. You cannot outrun all of them forever.

So you ward. Press space and throw a shove of will and light: it staggers whatever is closest, knocks it back, buys you the second you needed. A thing that is reeling cannot reach you. Ward the same one enough times and it doesn't just retreat — it comes apart. The ward spends the same legs a sprint does, so you cannot do it forever either. Warding and running are the same budget; spend it well.

⚠ if you would rather not be hunted

You can turn the creatures off, and you can turn damage off, in settings — see comfort & consent. Nothing about the deeper story requires you to be afraid for your body.

§ 06 — provisions

Almond water, and the reason not to trust it.

The one provision the maze reliably offers is almond water — a swallow restores your body and steadies your mind, and for a while afterward the lights hold calm. You will find it on shelves, and sometimes a cold, unbranded machine will vend you one if you ask it. Carry glowsticks and bandages when you can; a held light and a stopped wound both buy time.

⚠ don't trust the almond water on the deeper floors

Below the second layer the water can turn. It looks the same. It tastes almost the same. But a sour bottle heals nothing and takes a bite out of your mind instead — the maze offering you a hand and closing it. The wanderers wrote this warning on the walls long before you got here. Believe them.

§ 07 — the wish

Sometimes the dark will let you ask for something.

Now and then, near a presence — a listening quiet, not a thing that hunts — pressing speak opens a different kind of prompt. It asks you to state your request. You may ask for anything, in your own words.

Understand what this is. Your request is received. Whether it is heard is another matter, and not one the maze promises to settle in your favor. A wish is a door you knock on, not a lever you pull. Ask plainly. Ask for what you actually want. Then keep walking, because the answer, if it comes, comes on its own schedule.

Your request has been received. Whether it is heard is another matter.

§ 08 — the beacon

The one thing in here that reaches out there.

Everything else you do stays in the maze. The beacon does not. Press B and you push a signal out through the seam and into the real world — a message that arrives on a channel you chose, on a device that is not this one. It is the maze's single honest window: proof, to someone outside, that you were down here and pushing back.

You arm it in settings, under SIGNAL / BEACON. Point it at a topic or a web address you control, pick what it should do when it fires, and from then on B is a hand reaching up out of the floor. It runs warm — fire it and it needs a moment before it will fire again. Nothing else in the Backrooms leaves the Backrooms. This does.

arm it

Settings → SIGNAL / BEACON → set a target (a topic, or an https URL you own) and an effect.

fire it

Press B in the maze. The signal leaves. Somewhere outside, something you set up answers.

§ 09 — the atlas

806 is the first door. It is not the last.

Other doors are beginning to surface in the real world, and where they surface they go up on a shared map — the atlas — that anyone with the gift can open. There are two ways to reach a door. Stand where it stands: walk to the real place; presence is free and it is remembered most deeply. Or drop in from anywhere: paste a pin and reach a far door from your chair — but distance has a price, and what you leave behind is faint.

Nothing on the atlas is ever spent. Every visitor leaves a layer, and the door only deepens — the last to arrive reads more than the first ever could.

§ 10 — wanderers before you

You are reading a paper trail that never quite ends.

Scattered through the halls are scraps — notes, a page torn from a journal, a line scratched into the drywall by someone who came through before you. Walk up and press speak to read one. They are out of order, because the person who left them was out of order too, and together they tell a story you assemble by wandering. Finding them steadies you a little — proof you are not the only one who was ever here.

The longest thread belongs to a wanderer who signs only m. Read enough of it and you will start to suspect m. never found the way back up. Read all of it and you will know why this manual exists.

§ 11 — comfort & consent

You set the terms of your own descent.

This is meant to unsettle, not to harm. Everything that could frighten you is yours to turn down. In settings you can switch off the creatures entirely, switch off damage so nothing can hurt your body (peaceful), soften the film grain, quiet the ambience and music, and tune mouse sensitivity and head-bob until it sits right. None of it changes the story. All of it changes how hard the story leans on you.

peaceful

Damage off, creatures off. Walk the whole maze as a place, not a threat.

comfort

Grain down, motion down, sensitivity to taste. The dread is optional; the record is not.
