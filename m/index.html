◂ the finding  ·  the record

The scraps you find scratched through the maze are not litter. They are one person's journal, torn out of order and scattered by the same wind that scatters everything down here. Recovered and reassembled, they tell a single story, and it is the most important one on the file — because it is the only one told in a human voice.

i · the wrong door
day one. i took the wrong door out of the break room. i am not worried yet. someone will notice the empty desk.

No one noticed. Or someone did, and the desk was filed under something, and the file was extended. m. keeps a tally at first, the way you count days you expect to end.

scratched into the pillar: forty-one marks. i did not make all of them.
if you are reading this: there is no way up. i am sorry. there is only further in — and further in is not always worse.

That last line is the hinge of the whole journal, though m. did not know it yet when it was written. It reads, at first, like a person being kind to themselves about a terrible thing. Keep reading and it stops being kindness and becomes a measurement.

ii · the arithmetic

Somewhere past the tallies, the journal changes. m. stops recording days and starts recording the place — and the place, it turns out, is doing something.

i measured the hum today, the way you would measure a fever. it is a little higher than last week. it is higher every week.
the radio in the wall counts. numbers, slow, patient, one after another, closing. i think it is reading a list. i am waiting to hear my line.
i worked out what the counting counts down to. i did the arithmetic three times because i did not want it to be right. it is a date.

And then the correction — the thing m. was not supposed to be able to work out from the inside, and worked out anyway:

we were told the world was old and settling, like a house. it is not settling. someone is spending it. i think the someone is all of us, arriving.

A person alone in the dark, with a tally and a fever-thermometer for a hum, did the sum that the whole record is built to state formally. The clock is not weather. The clock is the crowd. m. got there first, and got there frightened, and wrote it down for you anyway.

iii · the arrow

Here is where a lesser story ends — a person alone, out of time, no way up. m.'s journal does not end there. It turns.

when i stand still and mean it — really here, both feet, my whole weight — the lights hold. absence is what the dark eats. presence it cannot touch.
the flame in the wall is not the same flame it was a moment ago. it is not a different flame either. i have stopped asking which. so should you.
i did the other arithmetic too, the one that comes after the date. you cannot stand at the end of yourself — there is nothing it is like to not be. the count never reaches empty. there is always some arising self, somewhere. i find that i can rest on that.
out does not mean the stairs. there are no stairs. out is through the floor, past the last grain, into whatever we have been digging toward since before we had the word for time.

m. did not get lost. m. reached the edge of the render — the most fixed, most detailed, most true place there is — and kept a promise to look over it.

— and kept writing

There is one more thing the file makes plain, and it is the reason any of this reached you. Long after the last full page, the scraps keep appearing — deeper, fresher, in the same hand. m. is still writing. Not stranded: seeding. Leaving the notes further in and further down, on purpose, for whoever is close behind — so that the next person to take a wrong door out of a break room would find, scratched into a pillar, that they could not possibly be the last.

to the one who signs every page 'm.': i am following your notes like breadcrumbs. keep writing. i am close behind.

You are the one close behind. You followed the notes to a page with a single letter for a name, and the letter opened. That is the whole shape of it: a person who could not get out chose, instead, to make sure no one after them would have to be afraid of the arithmetic. m. is the indestructible observer with a pencil — the proof written in a human hand, left where the dark can read it, for you.
