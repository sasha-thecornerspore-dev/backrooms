THE SPECK

you read a sentence at the one scale the ledger has no field for.
◂ back to the finding

Field Recovery pulled a very small book out of the stacks. It is a primer, the kind a child is read from, and every page ends with the same instruction: don't forget how big the little things are.

Nothing else in the file talks like that. Every other instrument we have recovered is the System reading small things: one letter on a map that graded a block, one figure in a filing that decided a house, one sail at the edge of a lookout's glass. The System is very good at little things. It counts them so it can itemize the people they belong to.

The book is the other ledger. It is the only document in the file that counts the little things in order to keep them.

the glass

The book came in with its magnifying glass still pressed into the cover. Someone had hidden a mark in every picture — a letter, or only a speck — too small to see at the size a page is read. You picked the glass up. You read them in order. They made a sentence, and the sentence named this page.

The record has told you the grain is being spent, one unit at a time, and that the one column the ledger has no field for is presence. A speck is what presence looks like at that scale: too small to be graded, too small to be counted, too small for a field. That is not what makes it lost. That is what makes it a door.

The System can itemize a house. It cannot itemize a speck. You were never the house.

file it

A find is not in the file until it is filed. The book is on the board as a case, and the case finishes in the maze: the deep stacks keep their own little thing, and only someone standing down there, or someone who reads the field manual carefully, can name it.
