## the backrooms 2.0.0 — the building has weight now

> you have no-clipped out of reality.

the maze is solid now. the things in it hunt, the floors connect, and who you are depends on how you came in.

### if you are new

a first-person horror maze: four floors stacked in one building, and, behind a door on the title screen, a real city block whose street is no longer on the map. there is no exit — only the way down. at the bottom, one door climbs back up to the lobby.

- **every run begins in the lobby**, level 0 — solo, pinned or online. nothing hunts there. below it, things do.
- **the front door**, a quiet line under the title's buttons, starts a solo run on level ∅ instead — the block, a real place. a form lies near the stoops: read it (**e**). the only way out is the gap the paperwork left (**f**).
- **play online** begins in the lobby, together: pick a room code, or send a friend an invite link, and fall into the same world.
- **pin a place:** paste a google maps link into the field above the buttons — a phone's short share link works too — and that place becomes the maze's seed. the same pin, the same halls, for anyone.
- click the view to take the mouse. **wasd** move · **shift** run — loud, and it runs out · **space** ward, hold to charge · **f** take, search, go through · **e** speak and read · **q** use · **x** set down · **tab** map · **c** whistle · **l** light · **1–6** slots · **enter** chat. the plate above your hands always says what **f** or **e** will do here.
- the game never pauses. settings, the map and the cards do not stop the world. close the window to quit; a solo run saves itself.
- something waits in the lobby that you cannot see. when `• • •  e · speak  • • •` appears, speak to it. wishes are real, from every copy of the game: what you say to the wall is posted publicly, and a person reads every one. a wish granted by hand drifts the world, in a later update, for everyone.
- light, almond water, m.'s pages and company steady your mind. the dark, the depth and the things eat at it. dying wakes you a floor up, and takes what was in your hand.
- too much? settings ⚙ → **creatures** off, **can take damage** off, **reduce flicker**. it is meant to unsettle, not to harm.
- everything else is in the field manual: <https://backrooms.thecornerspore.dev/manual/>

### install, on every system

- **windows** `The-Backrooms-Setup-2.0.0.exe` · **linux** `.AppImage` (updates itself) or `.deb` · **macos** one universal `.dmg` for intel and apple silicon
- macos builds are not notarized yet: the first time, right-click the app → **open** (or `xattr -cr "/Applications/The Backrooms.app"`)
- existing windows installs update to 2.0.0 on their own

### updates, your way

settings ⚙ → **updates**: **automatic** (downloads quietly, installs when you quit), **ask before downloading**, or **only when i check**. there is a **check for updates** button in every mode. automatic no longer restarts the game mid-run.

### things are solid

- furniture, machines, landmarks and the lost souls are bodies: you stop against them and slide along them. sprint into one and it is loud
- rooms are furnished like rooms: cabinets along real walls, chairs round a box, drums in the pump room, panels in the switch room
- the furnishing never blocks a doorway — the maze stays walkable
- don't want it? settings ⚙ → **solid furniture**

### the things hunt

- they **hear**: a sprint carries round corners, a walk barely does, and sound follows the halls, not straight through walls
- they **see** along the halls. walls break their sight, and so do the corners a furnished room forces on them
- they go alert, investigate the noise, hunt you, then search where they lost you. stay out of sight and keep quiet, and they give up
- each kind keeps one rule. some things only move when you are not looking. stay away from the ones that walk on all fours. bring a light to the pipes. on the deepest floor, the lights drop for a breath before the current finds you
- running down a hole does not shake a hunter. *it followed you down.*

### the fight

- **space** tap to ward, **hold** to charge a longer, harder push. warding and running spend the same legs
- a ward that lands just as a reeling thing turns back to face you hits harder
- **x** sets a thing down — a radio left talking draws them; a glowstick marks the way
- the polaroid flash blinds; sweet almond water makes you quiet; where things hunt, a bandage takes a still moment to wrap
- being hunted thickens the music and brings your heart into your ears

### the floors connect

- stairwells **up**, paired with the holes you fell through, and a one-way lift on level 1
- floors remember what you took, where you stood, and which machines you emptied; they restock only while you are away. *the machine has been refilled. by whom.*
- **tab** opens a pencil map of only what you have walked and seen, per floor, held while you keep moving
- the compass points at the ways onward you have actually seen, and back the way you came
- dying wakes you a floor above, beside the hole — lighter

### between encounters

- search cabinets, crates and drums: press **f** and keep still. mostly words, sometimes a find. not with something close
- some places are wrong every time: a figure standing where you just were, chairs that have been moved, water that keeps pace. none of it will touch you. a figure that stands still is the building; one that moves is not

### who you are — the form on the counter

behind the front door, on the block, the form is already half filled in. `/intake` reads it anywhere. *address: woodyear st. (there is no woodyear st.)*

nobody chooses a column. the first time you go through a way, the file writes you in by how you came in, and one line tells you where:

- **tenant** — *it has you at an address.* you gave it a name and nothing else. the street does not mind you; the deep does.
- **anchored** — *it has your body at a pin.* you fell through from a real place. drift too far from it and it pulls; a photograph quiets it for a while.
- **unnamed** — *it cannot spell you.* you gave it nothing to write. the things cannot keep hold of you. a presence will take a name, if you offer one.
- **processed** — *it opened a line on you.* online or on lan only: the first into an empty room. the lost souls can see the stamp.

over any of them, a layer: **thin**. *not all of you arrived.* drop into a room someone already walks, or die once the file has you, and the light starts reading you differently. ballast, from the deep machines, makes you whole.

the file also keeps a **status**, and this one is yours. it begins as a notice nobody answered. once the office can hear you, a presence offers three stamps under your request — **extension** · *let it stay open*, **compliance** · *close the file*, **litigation** · *contest it* — and each turns something inside out. each has its own closing, and none of them is a door out: a long stand in the dark on the deepest floor; thirteen pages given up unread; the seam. the office files once a real day, and the file follows you into every run and every room.

settings ⚙ → **your file**, or **enter** and `/status`, shows what the office has on you and how far your closing has come. `/intake` reads the form again. deep down, a machine may give you a slip that reads *notice 30150A*. a lost soul may tell you about *the thirteen who complied*. the rest is in the pages m. left lying around.

### together

- **c** whistles: friends hear it from where you stand, and it keeps a roll call. the things hear it too. answer each other, or the hall notices
- with a friend on your floor, a fatal hit lays you **down** for 25 s — they kneel beside you (**f**), light on, keeping still, and count you back
- a friend's light shelters you; a friend's ward pushes the things off you too; a photograph of a friend is evidence of them
- a set-down thing can carry a word from m.'s pages and an arrow, for whoever finds it
- a room travels as a link: **copy invite link** in the PLAY ONLINE box (**send invite** on a phone), or `/invite` in a game. whoever opens it sees *a door was left open* and one **JOIN ▶**. your place stays out of the link unless you ask for it, and asking says so first: *a pin in a link is a place in a link. don't share your home, and don't paste this on stream.*

### in the browser

the same game at <https://backrooms.thecornerspore.dev/play/>, installable and offline. wishes are posted and read like the desktop app's, and a granted wish reaches the browser when the site is next updated. photos are not saved, the beacon stays quiet, and lan play is not offered — an https page cannot reach your network. on a phone or tablet there is no chat, console or beacon. it picks up a new version the next time you open it. the release also carries the same game as a zip, `backrooms-web-2.0.0.zip`, for itch.io and other portals.

the site's front page and the browser game count page views with one cookieless counter (GoatCounter): which page, the site that sent you, your browser and operating system, your language, screen width and country. no cookie, no IP address stored, never a name, a wish or the chat — and no room code or pin: an invite link's are taken out of the address before the counter loads. the desktop app and the itch.io copy count nothing.

### for whoever runs the relay

- **redeploy it:** `cd relay && npx wrangler deploy`, from a full checkout (`relay/` imports a few files from `src/`). until then online play works as before and the new together verbs simply stay quiet. **host lan** from this release carries all of them. update together — mixed versions in one room fall back to the old behaviour.
- **wishes need the `GITHUB_TOKEN` secret:** `npx wrangler secret put GITHUB_TOKEN` from `relay/` — a fine-grained token on the repository with issues read and write, nothing else. every copy of 2.0.0 sends wishes to the relay's `POST /wish`, and no build carries a token any more. without the secret the route answers `closed`, and wishes are lost without a word to the player. `IP_SALT` is optional.
- **revoke the old wish token before publishing 2.0.0.** every earlier installer carried the token stored as the `ISSUES_TOKEN` secret inside `app.asar`, and those installers stay downloadable. revoke that token (github.com/settings/tokens), then delete the `ISSUES_TOKEN` repository secret. make the relay's `GITHUB_TOKEN` a new fine-grained token: this repository only, issues read and write, nothing else.
- **the Gate:** this deploy runs the `v2` migration, which adds the `Gate` durable object. it rate-limits `/wish` and `/maps` per sender, by a salted hash of the IP (an IPv6 address by its /64), caps `/wish` at 300 a day from everyone together, and forgets it all a day after the last request. `/wish` answers only the site and the itch CDN (and the desktop app's main process, which sends no origin).
- **short map links:** a phone's `maps.app.goo.gl` link is resolved by the relay's `GET /maps`, which returns only the coordinates. until the relay is redeployed, those links read *that place cannot be found*.
- invocation logs are off for the whole worker, so the relay logs nothing about a request.
