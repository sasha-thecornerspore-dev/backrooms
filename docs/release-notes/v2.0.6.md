## the backrooms 2.0.6 — the field terminal

the game had no way to look back over what you had found. a page read once was gone until you found it again, a radio group went by, a lost soul's line faded off the screen. now there is a small terminal that reads back what the file has of you. you can look up and down now too, and a case's notes open only for the answer that earns them.

**on windows with 2.0.3, 2.0.4 or 2.0.5:** your copy cannot update itself: its update check fails. download `The-Backrooms-Setup-2.0.6.exe` below and run it once, over the old install. your saves stay. from 2.0.6 on it updates itself again.

### what changed for players

- **the field terminal.** press the key left of 1, or type `/term` on the line (on touch: **TYPE**, then `/term`). a small screen opens on the right, or at the top on a phone: *the terminal reads back what the file has of you. it never writes down an answer.* nothing pauses while it is open: the building goes on around you, the way it does while you read the map. a way, a death or settings folds it, and so do esc, the same key again, `exit`, the hint at its top, or a click outside it. a hit does not, as it does not fold the line: what you go on typing stays in it, and never reaches your hands. tab finishes a word, the arrows bring back what you typed, and whatever the building says while it is open is copied in, faint.
- **what it reads back.** m.'s pages you have read, in the order you found them, across runs and rooms (`pages`, `read <name>`) — a copy, which counts nothing and steadies nothing; a page you gave up stays sealed. the station's groups you heard since you woke (`radio`): *the station counts from the start each time you wake. so does this.* the words on the things you picked up, and whose (`words`); what the lost and the drawers told you, and the note by the cone (`voices`); the sights and the dens the film caught (`sights`); all of it floor by floor (`floors`, `where`), and searched by a word (`grep`). the building's last lines on this floor (`log`): *what the building said, lately. it keeps no more than this.* and a margin you write yourself (`jot`, `margin`, `strike`): *your lines. the file does not read them.*
- **it learns its words as you find things.** until you have found something of a kind, the terminal does not know the word for it, and says so the way it says so for a word it will never know: *the terminal does not know that word. type help.* `help` lists only what it knows. the first time the file keeps something for it, the building says *the file keeps a copy. the terminal reads it back.*
- **the open case, as far as you have read it.** `notes` reads each instrument you have filed and its note, then the one open now, what it asks for and how it is read; nothing past it. once every instrument is read it holds the closing file and its key: **the closing key no longer appears on the screen** when you file the last instrument. instead: *the case is read. the terminal keeps its closing file.* `notes` is now the one place in the game it shows. `notes <case>` reads another case without opening it.
- **filing waits.** `/file` on the line and `file` in the terminal go through the same wait: *reading it against the record.*, and the answer no sooner than a moment later. a wrong answer holds the next one off for four seconds, then eight, sixteen, and half a minute at most; a right one puts the wait back. filed again too soon: *the office takes no filing from you yet.* the wrong-answer line is still the only one: *the file does not answer to that.* an answer is never echoed back, kept in the terminal's history, or stored: the terminal shows a filing as `› file · · ·`. filed from the terminal while you stand on the floor the instrument names, the file notes where — *filed where it was meant to be read. the file notes where.* — and `notes` marks it *filed from its floor.* it changes nothing else.
- **forget.** what the terminal reads back is kept on this device — never in your save, never sent — and outlives SOLO and rooms. settings → your file → **forget what the terminal kept** clears it (press twice). your file, your save and your case marks stay.
- **look up and down.** the mouse, once it is grabbed, and the drag on a phone now tilt your view as well as turn you: the ceiling tiles, the carpet, the thing at the end of the hall. nothing above or below you is in reach; you look, that is all. your light goes where you look. a fall, a waking and lying down bring the view back level, and so do the arrow keys while the mouse is loose. settings has **Vertical look** (*look up and down too*) to switch it off if a tilting view swims, and **Invert vertical look** (*push up to look down*).
- **a case's notes are sealed.** the note an instrument gives when it is read, and the closing file, now open only with the answer that reads it, the same in the game's console as on the case board. a case filed in the game keeps its key on this device, so `notes` opens it again (in the browser copy at `/play/`, the board does too). the newest case on the board, R-14, is the first to be sealed; the older four read as before.
- **the version on the title screen.** the desktop app shows its version in the title's bottom-right corner as it opens, and at the foot of settings without waiting for settings to open. the browser copy has no version to show, and shows none.
- **a litigation file that has closed** now reads both its steps done on every later run (`/status`, settings → your file); before, a new run read *the claim, typed* and *the beacon, pushed* as not done under a file that was already closed.

### update together

nothing new goes over the wire, and a room can mix 2.0.5 and 2.0.6. the terminal reads only what your own game already has; it never shows a friend's lines, and nothing it keeps is sent. the case board on the site shares the cases' record with the browser copy at `/play/` only: the desktop app and the itch.io copy keep their own, as they always did. on 2.0.5, a sealed case still files, but its note does not open in the game: the board opens it. vertical look is drawn on your own screen only; a friend sees you turn, as before.

a solo run saved on 2.0.5 continues where you left it. the first time it loads, the pages it has read go into the terminal's record, without the floor they were found on (*an earlier walk*).

### install, on every system

- **macos** `The-Backrooms-2.0.6-universal.dmg` (or `.zip`), one build for intel and apple silicon
  - macos 13 (ventura) or later. on macos 12, keep 2.0.2, or play in the browser
  - not notarized yet: the first time, right-click the app → **open** (or `xattr -cr "/Applications/The Backrooms.app"`)
- **windows** `The-Backrooms-Setup-2.0.6.exe` · **linux** `The-Backrooms-2.0.6-x86_64.AppImage` (updates itself) or `The-Backrooms-2.0.6-amd64.deb`
- windows installs on 2.0.3, 2.0.4 and 2.0.5 take this one install by hand, over the old one (see the top). appimage installs on 2.0.x move to 2.0.6 on their own, or when you ask, whichever you chose in settings; from 2.0.6 on, windows installs do the same
- the browser copy picks it up the next time you open it (sometimes the time after): its offline cache moves to `backrooms-pwa-v14` for the four new files

### for whoever runs the relay and the site

- **nothing to redeploy by hand:** the relay and the lan server are unchanged, and nothing new goes over the wire.
- **the Windows build checks itself:** Build & Release now fails a Windows build whose `app-update.yml` carries a publisher name, before anything is uploaded. that name is what kept 2.0.3 to 2.0.5 from updating.
- **a release run is safer to repeat:** a leftover draft that points at another commit is deleted and made again at this one, and a run a person cancels publishes nothing.
- **the site's pages are copied by hand:** when this goes out, put `docs/manual.html` at `manual/index.html` on gh-pages. it now covers the field terminal and vertical look.
- **the case board:** R-14 goes out sealed once this release is out (the author's step), so the game that can open its notes is out first.
