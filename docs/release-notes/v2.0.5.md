## the backrooms 2.0.5 — the floors keep count

a floor was the same floor every time you came back to it, and the things forgot every way you had got away from them. now a floor can feel different for a visit, the things learn how you get away, the radio hisses when something is near, the film shows you where a thing lives, and a phone can type.

### what changed for players

- **some visits, a floor feels different.** now and then, about one arrival in three, a floor below the block is not quite as you left it, for that visit, and says so once: *the lights are out here.* (the panels dark, a few lamps still burning, your own light matters), *it is very quiet.* (the hum a little lower, the floor's moods and murmurs about half as often), or *someone came through here before you.* (some drawers already searched, and on the lobby some chairs moved). never the same way two visits running, never your first time in the lobby, never on the block, and never in a shared room. nothing else about the floor moves: the same maze, rooms, items and things, and the things still see by the floor's own light. the line is one of the arrival's two: on a floor you come back to it comes just after the floor's name (off the lift, just after the lift's own line); on a first visit the floor's own lines keep their places, and the lift's, and it is said only if they leave room: if not, the feeling holds, unsaid; climbing out to the lobby it is said in place of *the carpet remembers you.* a drawer someone else searched is searched for that visit only.
- **the things learn your habits.** each floor remembers how you got away from its hunts: standing still in the dark, a ward, running, or the flash. get away the same way three times on one floor and its things change against it for the rest of the run: they wait longer where they lost you, keep going the way you were heading, or come back sooner from a ward or the flash. the building says so once for each, starting with *it has learned to wait.* get away some other way and it works as it always did. only alone: nothing is learned in a room, on the block, or with the creatures switched off.
- **the radio warns you, after a fashion.** carried and on, it hisses under its tune when something is near you through the halls, louder as the thing closes by the way you would have to walk: a thing just behind a wall is only as loud as the long way round to it. the hiss is the same in both ears, so it never says which way, and once something is coming for you the hiss does not die down, but it no longer rises for that thing, and one that followed you down brings none. the price is unchanged and now firm: with the radio on you cannot hide by standing still. the numbers station, its groups, its lines, its timing and the floor's roll call are as 2.0.4 had them, and a radio set down still talking does not hiss for you.
- **the film is worth what it shows.** photograph one of the things close up (a few steps off, in frame, nothing between you) and after its caption the film develops it: *the film develops the hound, and the hall it keeps to. you mark it on your map.* its den goes on your pencil map as ⌂: the crossing of the halls where it lives on that floor, or, for one that followed you down, the spot where you caught it. each kind develops once a visit. the den is yours alone and saved with your map, and it costs the arrival none of its lines. the captions, the anchored pin and the tenant's address develop as before.
- **phones can type, and send a beacon.** a **TYPE** button appears whenever the line can open, alone or in a room: tap it and the line opens with your phone's keyboard, so the chat, `/status`, `/intake` and the field console's cases (`/recover`, `/cases`, `/file`) are no longer keyboard-only. the keyboard's enter key sends; sending it empty puts the line away. once a beacon is set in settings, a **BEACON** button does what **b** does (in a phone's browser, as in any browser, the webhook goes quiet).
- **the touch buttons keep clear.** they no longer sit on the gauges, the item slots, ✕ or the gear, at any size: a small phone held upright had CALL under the slot bar, and a larger one on its side had SPEAK under the gear. TYPE and BEACON stand in a column of their own wherever there is room, so the six never move when the two come and go. desktop and keyboard play are unchanged.

### update together

nothing new goes over the wire, and a room can mix 2.0.4 and 2.0.5. what would need everyone to agree stays off in a room: a floor never feels different there, and the things never learn. the radio's hiss is worked out on your own machine from what is near you, and your dens stay on your own map (a friend's map does not get them).

a solo run saved on 2.0.4 continues where you left it, with nothing learned yet and no dens on its map. what a floor has learned is an optional part of its memory, and a den is a pin like the others: opened again on 2.0.4, the run goes on, a den shows as a plain dot, and the floors forget what they learned the next time 2.0.4 saves.

### install, on every system

- **macos** `The-Backrooms-2.0.5-universal.dmg` (or `.zip`), one build for intel and apple silicon
  - macos 13 (ventura) or later. on macos 12, keep 2.0.2, or play in the browser
  - not notarized yet: the first time, right-click the app → **open** (or `xattr -cr "/Applications/The Backrooms.app"`)
- **windows** `The-Backrooms-Setup-2.0.5.exe` · **linux** `The-Backrooms-2.0.5-x86_64.AppImage` (updates itself) or `The-Backrooms-2.0.5-amd64.deb`
- windows and appimage installs on 2.0.x move to 2.0.5 on their own, or when you ask, whichever you chose in settings
- the browser copy picks it up the next time you open it (sometimes the time after): its offline cache moves to `backrooms-pwa-v13` for the four new files

### for whoever runs the relay and the site

- **nothing to redeploy:** the relay and the lan server are unchanged. **Build & Release** starts the browser copy's build on the tag, as it did for 2.0.4.
- **a mac build that never starts no longer holds a release back:** macOS builds in a job of its own now. the release waits for it, so its files go out with the rest, but goes out on windows and linux however it ends; a release with no mac files gets them when that job is re-run.
- **a switch for the feelings:** `world.json` ships `"feelings": true`; set it to `false` and no floor feels different anywhere (the browser copy on its next site deploy, the desktop app with its next release).
- **the site's pages are copied by hand:** when this goes out, put `docs/manual.html` at `manual/index.html` on gh-pages. it now covers the feelings, the things learning, the radio's hiss, the den on the map and the two new touch buttons.
