§ 01 — the gift

You would not be reading this if you could not enter.

The Backrooms are not a place you build. They are a place you fall through — a seam in a wall that a moment ago was solid. Most people walk past the seam their whole lives and never see it. A few can. That is the gift, and it is the only prerequisite. If the runes on the front page resolved into words for you, you already have it.

What waits on the other side is an interior that does not end: humming light, damp carpet, walls that go on being walls in every direction. Under it, three more floors, each older and darker than the one above, and at the bottom a door that climbs back up to the top. You are not the first down here. You will not be the last. This manual is what earlier wanderers wished someone had handed them.

It is written for your first ten minutes and for your fortieth hour. Read the next three sections before you go in. Read the rest when the building starts asking questions you cannot answer.

Reality inside is reality outside — the same, recursively, all the way down. You have not left the world. You have gone one layer deeper into it.

⚠ the building does not stop for you

There is no pause. Settings, the map, a page in your hands, the chat line, a request to a presence — none of them stop the world, and the things keep moving while you read. Only the fade between floors holds everything still. To stop, close the window; a solo run saves itself as it closes.

§ 02 — how a run begins

You wake in the lobby. The block is behind the front door.

Every run begins in the lobby, Level 0: yellow wallpaper, damp carpet, the hum. Solo or online, pinned or not, nothing hunts you there. Online and LAN runs start everyone there together.

Level ∅, the block, is reached on purpose. Under the title's buttons sits a quiet line, the front door, and it starts a solo run outside instead: a real inner-block park in Harlem Park, West Baltimore, under a flat grey sky. Nothing hunts there either. There is a form lying on open ground, and there is one gap that leads down. Once you take it you cannot come back.

From the lobby the way is down: 0, then 1, then 2, then 3. The bottom floor has a door that climbs back out to the lobby, so the descent is a ring, not a ladder. The block sits outside the ring. You pass through it once, if you choose to.

level 0. somewhere. you are here.— the title screen

The title screen

A slow camera drifts down a lobby corridor behind the title. It is the same corridor for everyone. Under the placard:

Your name, up to 24 characters, remembered between sessions. It becomes your name in the chat (with its own colour), the pitch of your whistle, the signature on anything you leave behind, and the first line of a form someone else will fill in. Anchors — falling through a real place

A pin seeds the world from that place. Everyone who pins the same spot falls into the same maze — solo. A room draws its maze from the spot's neighbourhood instead (below). While anchored, your level plate shows a drift — how far you have wandered from where this floor set you down — and settings gains locate your body. The building speaks of it on every floor: your body remains at …, and later, you are very far from your body now.

A phone's share button gives a short link with no place in it, so the game asks the relay where it leads: looking for that place… Only the link is sent, never the words a share sheet puts around it, and only the two numbers come back. A place that cannot be read says that place cannot be found, and the run starts without a pin.

Your pin is yours. Solo, nothing sends it anywhere on its own — a short link goes to the relay once, to be read, as above, and locate your body opens Google Maps at it, when you press it; that is all. A room — online or LAN, host or joiner — is told only its neighbourhood: the pin rounded to two decimals, a cell about 1.1 km north to south and 0.86 km east to west at Baltimore's latitude. Open a room with a pin and its world is drawn from that neighbourhood, not from your door; the relay keeps a room's world for good and hands it to everyone who enters the code, and the neighbourhood can be read back out of it. The PLAY ONLINE box says so before you press ENTER, and the chat says it once more as you arrive: this room is drawn from your pin's neighbourhood. anyone with the code can see which square kilometre — to someone who already has a few of your addresses, that is the door. A square kilometre is not your door, but it picks your door out of a short list: if anyone with the code might already have a few addresses for you, play that room without a pin. Join a room someone else drew and the others get a mark of your pin's neighbourhood — enough to tell whether you came through the same part of town, and, with marks from two rooms, or a guess at your city, enough to read roughly where. An invite link with your place in it carries the same neighbourhood, and the same square kilometre. Beyond the short link's lookup and locate your body, only a beacon aimed at your own https address ever carries the pin itself.

A room opened with a pin from 2.0.1 or earlier was drawn from that exact pin, and the relay still keeps it: a room's world always wins, so a newer copy cannot take it back. Give that room up and open a new code; a newer copy that finds itself in one drawn from its own pin says so: this room was drawn from your pin itself, by an older copy. anyone with the code can find the door. leave the code and open a new one. A 2.0.1 copy still sends its exact pin's seed whenever it joins a room (a new room is drawn from it) and still sends its finer mark — update before you bring a pin into a room. A newer copy matches that mark only when the older copy came in through a newer invite; walking and chat are unchanged.

Nothing in the game reads your location on its own. You type the pin, or you don't.

Three kinds of world

solo, no pin

The building moves. Rooms far behind you are forgotten and grow back different. Your map will notice before you do.

solo, with a pin

A fixed world, seeded from the place. Come back tomorrow and the halls are where you left them.

online or lan

The room's world, fixed. The first person in decides it — with a pin, from its neighbourhood; everyone after inherits it.

anchored worlds hold their shape. unanchored worlds forget you were ever there.

§ 03 — your hands

Everything you can do, and the key that does it.

Click the view first. That grabs the mouse; without it the mouse does nothing. You look left and right only — there is no looking up or down, and the mouse buttons and wheel do nothing. Chat, a card, a request, a fall between floors or being laid down (online) will release the mouse; click again to take it back. Esc releases it too.

Move. Forward, back, strafe. Backing up and sidestepping are slower than walking forward. The carpet is soft; your footsteps aren't.

Look. Mouse once it is grabbed. The arrow keys turn you only while the mouse is not grabbed. Sensitivity is in settings.

Run. Faster, and loud, and it spends your legs — the same stamina a ward costs. Run dry and you walk until it comes back. You cannot run with the map up.

It leaves out N, Esc, the arrow keys, and clicking the view to grab the mouse. They are all above.

⚠ small habits of the keys

Nothing you press reaches the game while a text box has your cursor. F E Space Tab Q X C fire once per press, however long you hold them. Switching to another window lets go of every key you were holding. While you lie down, only C, Enter and Tab still work.

On a phone or tablet

Touch controls appear only on touch screens, and they feed the same actions.

  • The stick, bottom left. Push it a little to walk; push it all the way to the rim going forward and you run.
  • Look by dragging anywhere on the open view. Left and right only.
  • Buttons on the right, from the bottom up: ACT (F — take · descend), USE (Q), WARD (Space — tap, or hold to charge exactly like the key), SPEAK (E — talk · wish), LIGHT (L), CALL (C).
  • The map is the folded paper corner under your gauges. Tap it to open; tap the sheet to fold it away.
  • Your hands: tap a slot to choose it; tap ✕ to set it down. On a narrow phone the slots move up under the gauges.
  • Cards: tap to put one back. Choices are tappable lines; a question shows yes · e and no · esc as lines you tap. SPEAK says yes; ACT or WARD folds a page.

Touch has no way into the chat or the console, no beacon, and no track change; the music switch lives in settings. If you have a keyboard attached, those keys work. Installed as an app, the game runs landscape and full-screen.

The building speaking

One line at a time appears in the middle of the screen, on a small notice, for a few seconds each. Some are the floor talking to itself. Some are about you. The important ones push in — a hit, a warning — and the idle ones are dropped when anything else needs saying. Read them. They are the closest thing down here to instructions.

The whole screen speaks too: a red wash when you are hurt, violet when your mind runs low, black while you lie down, a black fade between floors that holds until the new floor has drawn, a warm flash for a photograph, a shake for something heavy. Reduce flicker in settings softens the flash and the shake.

Anything you read arrives as a card in your hands. While it is up you cannot move or act — and the world keeps going. A hit or a change of floor folds it away.

§ 06 — body, mind & light

Sanity is the resource you did not know you were spending.

❤ HP — your body

The things hurt you on contact, harder the deeper you are. A hit jolts the screen red, and for a moment nothing else can touch you. It also folds the map, closes a card, spoils a bandage wrap (you keep the bandage), and leaves an × on your map.

Your body mends itself, slowly, once nothing has touched you for a few seconds.

◐ SAN — your mind

The dark eats at it, so does depth, so does being hunted, so does a thing you cannot stop looking at. Let it run low and violet closes in at the edges; lower still and the whispering starts, and the building finds more ways to frighten you.

Nothing kills you when it is empty. It only makes everything worse.

What steadies your mind: your flashlight, a sip of sweet almond water, a page you have not read before, a word found in a drawer, a photograph, a thing you pushed until it came apart, and — best of all — a friend standing near you. What takes it: the dark, the deeper floors, being hunted, looking too long at the wrong thing, sour water, the places that are wrong, the friend who went quiet.

rule i learned today: the almond water is real. drink it. the dark drinks you back if you do not.— m., a page

Your legs

Stamina is one pool for two things: running and warding. A full pool is a few breaths of running. It refills when you walk or stand, but not while you hold a charged ward. Running headlong into something heavy knocks a little out of you, and it is loud. Sweet almond water and ballast fill it at once. Do not sprint away from a thing and then try to push it off — there will be nothing left in your legs to push with.

Your light

Press L — flashlight on. It is on when you arrive. Light steadies you; turn it off and the dark leans in. On the lobby and the habitable zone the building lights itself. On pipe dreams and the electrical station there are no lights at all: your beam is the only one, and on those floors the things see less far in the dark — unless you are the light. Sometimes it is worth being dark. Often it is not.

If you can stand in the dark beside a friend whose light is on, you are harder to see. See together.

Your heart

When something is hunting you, your heartbeat comes into your ears, faster as it closes. The floor's own music thickens with it: a beat comes in, it darkens and gets louder. When you lose what was following you, the music takes a long breath before it sounds like itself again. If you can hear your pulse, it is already near. it is close.

Everything goes dark

When your body gives out, the screen fades — everything goes dark. — and you wake one floor up, beside the hole you fell through, facing it. On the lobby and on the block you wake where you are.

  • Whatever was in your hand is gone. your hands opened. Switch to something cheap before a fight you might lose.
  • You come back a little less. you wake beside the hole you fell through. something of you stayed down there. Your body's ceiling drops a little each time, so the HP bar never quite fills again. Every floor you set foot on for the first time gives some of it back.
  • The machines on that floor are empty until you next take a way. the tray is empty.
  • The floor remembers where you fell. the floor remembers you less.
  • Once the file has you, dying changes how it has you. See thin.
  • Online, with a friend on your floor, a killing blow may lay you down instead.
§ 07 — the descent

Down is the only direction that means anything.

The floors are stacked in one building. You go down through a way — a torn doorway in the world with a cold blue rim that breathes, a ring on the floor, and a beam that carries through the fog. Walk up to one, read the prompt, press F. The screen goes black, you fall, and the floor below says its name. Fall through a hole and you land near the same spot one floor down.

level ∅ · the block

harlem park, west baltimore. a plan cleared the street out of the block and left this.

Reached on purpose, through the front door on the title screen, solo: outdoors, the one real place in the game — an inner-block park ringed by rowhouse backs — formstone, brick, plywood with a sprayed house number, front doors sealed with grey block, black windows, a few lit ones, marble stoops. Trash, tires, weeds. No ceiling, no things, no machines, no pages, no map. A form lies on open ground near the stoops; press E to read it. Someone started filling it out.

the front doors are sealed with block. the only way out is the gap the paperwork left.

Walk to the gap and press F — f · no-clip out — level 0. Nothing leads back. Listen while you are here. The block has a few things to say about its doors, and about a street that ran where you are standing and runs on somewhere else. What it is, and why it is a real place, is the subject of the record.

⚠ a hole does not shake a hunter

If something is hunting you when you drop, it may come down after you a few seconds later. it followed you down. Lose it before you go, or be ready when you land. Not every way lets it follow.

Every floor remembers you

What you took stays taken. Drawers you searched stay searched. What you set down lies where you left it. Come back to a floor and it says so: you have been on this floor before. — and tells you how many ways down are on your map. An emptied vending machine gives again only after you have been away from its floor a good while. It never refills while you watch.

the machine has been refilled. by whom.

§ 08 — the map & the compass

You start drawing. It is the only way to know you are moving.

Press Tab (on touch, the paper corner under your gauges) and a cream sheet rises over the lower part of your view. It is drawn in pencil, and it holds only what you have walked — the halls right around you as you pass, nothing through walls, nothing down the parallel corridor you never entered. Over the strokes, glyphs for what you have seen: a way at the end of a long hall goes on the sheet even if you never reached it. Each floor keeps its own sheet. There is no map on the block.

You hold the map; it does not stop anything. While it is up you walk at half pace, cannot run, cannot act and cannot ward. A hit knocks it out of your hands. Tab or Esc folds it.

§ 09 — the building

If the lights hold, something is holding them.

The building talks. Every so often a floor murmurs a line of its own, and repeats its hint about the way down. The lights flicker, a little on the lobby, a great deal by the electrical station. When they suddenly hold steady, it is because something is holding them: water you drank, a find you used, a file you closed.

Things that happen

On the four floors below the block the building has moods. Lights go out down a hall and come back. A far door slams. The hum stops. A cold spot. Below the lobby, footsteps that keep your pace, and a figure crossing an intersection far ahead. They come more often the deeper you are and the lower your mind runs, and never while you are reading or speaking. None of them can hurt you — a cold spot takes a little of your mind, and that is all.

footsteps. not yours. they keep your pace, and stop when you stop.

Places that are wrong

Some places are wrong every time you pass them. A chair that was not turned before. Your own light, for a breath. Something behind you on the trail you just walked. These are hauntings. A haunting never touches you, never comes toward you, and never happens while something real is near. Each one takes a little of your mind and goes on the map as a plain dot.

So remember the one rule the wanderers agree on: a figure that stands still is the building. One that moves is not.

the chairs have been moved. nobody moved them.

Furniture, rooms, landmarks

You walk around furniture, machines, landmarks and lost souls; papers, pallets and weeds you walk over. Clutter hugging a wall you can edge past, slowly — you edge past the transformer. it is warm. Sprinting into something heavy is loud. A thing that is hunting you is solid too: it does not let you through. If you would rather pass through everything but walls, turn off solid furniture in settings.

Rooms are dressed like rooms — offices on the lobby, storerooms on the habitable zone, pump rooms in the pipes, switch rooms on the electrical station — and their furniture is cover. Furniture alone does not hide you from a thing's eyes, but a dressed room forces corners, and corners do. The cabinets, crates, drums and panels in them can be searched. See drawers.

Rarely, a landmark: something solid and wrong you will not forget. The first time you see one it says what it is and goes on your map as ✶. Steer by them.

a payphone. the cord has been cut. it is ringing.

§ 10 — the things

Below zero, you are not alone, and not everything is a wanderer.

From the habitable zone down, things move that are not people and were never people. Each has a rule. The rule is learnable, and the building will usually say it to you once, at the worst possible moment. What follows is what wanderers have seen and lived to write down.

What they all do

  • They hear. A sound carries through the halls, never through walls, quieter the farther it goes. Walking is quiet. Running is loud. A ward is louder. A whistle is louder still. A thing that hears you stops, turns toward the sound — something, far off, stops. — and comes to look.
  • They see. Walls block their sight; furniture does not, but corners do. On the dark floors they see much less — unless your flashlight is on. Right up close, they need no line at all.
  • They hunt. it has seen you. Break the line — a corner, a door, a dressed room — and keep quiet, and after a while it goes to search where it last saw you. you have lost it. it is still looking. Then it gives up.
  • They follow you down. A hunter close behind you when you take a hole or a stair may arrive a few seconds after you.
  • They hunt movement. Once the file has you, a person who stands completely still and silent in the dark — light off, radio off — becomes, after a breath, very hard to find. Before the file has you, there is nowhere to hide. See the file.

if the lights go out, stop moving. it hunts movement.— a lost soul

§ 14 — the wish

Sometimes the dark will let you ask for something.

A presence is a listening quiet, not a thing that hunts. You cannot see one. Near one, a line appears above your hands — • • • e · speak • • • — and that is all. There is always one close to where you first arrive in the lobby. Elsewhere they are scattered thinly, and you notice one only when you are almost on top of it — unless your radio is on, which lets you hear one from much farther off.

Press E and a prompt opens: state your request. You may ask for anything, in your own words. Under the box, faint lines sometimes suggest what you might say; some of them you can tap. Press submit. The reply prints, and the prompt closes on its own a few seconds later.

⚠ the prompt keeps your keys

Enter makes a new line; it does not send. Esc does nothing while the cursor is in the box. Use submit or cancel, or click outside the box first. And the world does not wait while you type.

Understand what this is. Your request is received. Whether it is heard is another matter, and not one the maze promises to settle in your favour. A wish is a door you knock on, not a lever you pull. Ask plainly. Ask for what you actually want. Then keep walking, because the answer, if it comes, comes on its own schedule.

Wishes are real. From the desktop app, the browser or a phone, what you say to the wall is posted publicly, and a person reads it — every one. The spirits answer, or they don't. Some are granted, by hand, and a granted wish changes the world, quietly, for everyone: a colour, the fog, the hum, what lies around. It reaches the browser when the site is next updated and the desktop app in the update that follows. The Backrooms never changes obviously. It drifts.

Because it is posted and read, write it the way you would write on a wall: put nothing real about yourself in it.

A presence also listens for names, from those the file could not spell; give it one and it will ask how that is spelled. And once the office can hear you, a presence is where you choose the word your file is kept under. See the file.

your request has been received. whether it is heard is another matter.

§ 16 — together

It is easier to be lost together.

Online or on a LAN, other people appear as pale figures with nameplates and a small health bar under each name. Each of the things you can do for each other below also works the other way.

stay close

A friend a few steps away steadies your mind — the best medicine there is. Stand together long enough and even that stops helping, until you have been apart a while. you have been standing together a long time. it stops helping.

call out

C (or CALL) whistles two notes at a pitch only yours. Everyone on your floor hears it from where you stand, and their chat says how far and which way: maddie whistles · near ↗. The hall counts who has answered lately: three of you. A friend who goes a long while without a whistle, a word, or standing near you has gone quiet, and it weighs on you. The things hear every call. Alone, the hall keeps the sound. Usually.

share the light

Once you have taken your first way down, stand dark in a friend's flashlight — a few steps from them, nothing between you — and the things have a harder time finding you, and the dark eats you slower. Switch yours off and you will hear whose light it is: flashlight off — you stand in maddie's light. It never helps the one carrying it.

push for each other

When a friend on your floor wards, it lands on your things too, from where they stand. If you were in front of their push when it met something, it steadies you. maddie pushes the dark off you.

evidence

A photograph of a friend develops what the file wrote on them — and reaches them. For a while their mind cannot sink below a certain floor, and everyone sees them solid, however thin they have worn. someone has evidence of you. you are harder to erase.

see each other

Your light warms the friends it falls on. Chat floats over your head as speech. /me emotes. A friend who is down reads name · down.

Down, not dead

Online, with a friend on your floor who is not down themselves, a blow that would kill you lays you down instead. everything goes dark. you are still here. somewhere, someone may notice. Your light goes out, a black veil comes over the screen, your heart beats slowly. You cannot move or use anything, and your mind slips. You can still whistle, chat and read the map — calling is how someone finds you.

You have 25 seconds. If nobody comes, it is a death, and a hard one: nobody came. If everyone on the floor is down, nobody can come.

To count a friend back: face them, close, and read f · stay with them. Press F to kneel. Your light comes on — it is theirs now — and you hold still while you count them back; the veil over their screen lifts as you do. your hands are on them. A step, Space, Esc or F again lets go, and the count starts over. You are the one in danger while you kneel. Stay. A photograph does it faster.

you stayed. you counted them back.

§ 17 — online & lan

Pick a room code and share it.

Type your name on the title screen, press PLAY ONLINE ⬡, choose a room code (up to 32 characters; the box starts with a random room-… you can keep), and press ENTER ▶. The first person in decides the world, and their pin's neighbourhood with it — never the pin itself; everyone after inherits it. An online run starts in the lobby and is never saved. How you arrive is written down.

from the box

Opens a small form: the server address (it starts as ws://localhost:8765) and the room (backrooms). Type what your friend's plate says, on the same network, and press ENTER ▶.

§ 18 — the beacon

The one thing in here that reaches out there.

Everything else you do stays in the maze, or goes only where the office sends it. The beacon goes where you send it. Press B and you push a signal out through the seam and into the real world — a message that arrives on a channel you chose, on a device that is not this one. It is the maze's single honest window: proof, to someone outside, that you were down here and pushing back.

You arm it in settings, under SIGNAL / BEACON. Choose an effect — an ntfy.sh topic, a Discord webhook, or a custom https address you control — and set the target. From then on B is a hand reaching up out of the floor. It runs warm: fire it and it needs a few seconds before it will fire again. It fires only from the desktop app; in a browser, the beacon goes quiet. It is the only thing in here that reaches a place you chose.

arm it

§ 19 — the atlas

§ 20 — save & continue

A solo run keeps itself. A shared one does not.

A solo run saves on its own, every few seconds, on every way you take, every death, every thing you set down or pick up, and when you close the window. CONTINUE ▶ on the title screen puts you back.

kept

Your floor, where you stand and which way you face, your body and its ceiling, your hands (a sour bottle stays sour), the pages you have read, your world and your pin, what every floor remembers of you, your map, your deaths, and who the file has you as.

not kept

Your mind and your legs start fresh. So does where the numbers station was in its count, and anything you were partway through toward the oldest ending. Close the window and that work is gone.

Your status is not part of the save. The word you are filed under, its closing, and the pages you have given up are kept by the office, not by the run — they follow you into every solo run, every room, and survive pressing SOLO.

⚠ before you press SOLO or the front door

SOLO ▶ and the front door start over and overwrite your solo save without asking. Online and LAN runs are never saved. The save lives in this browser or this app; it does not travel between them.

§ 22 — comfort & consent

You set the terms of your own descent.

This is meant to unsettle, not to harm. Everything that could frighten you is yours to turn down. In settings you can switch off the creatures entirely, switch off damage so nothing can hurt your body, turn on reduce flicker for softer light dips, a gentler flash and less shaking, turn off film grain, particles and head-bob, quiet the ambience and music, and tune mouse sensitivity until it sits right. None of it changes the story. All of it changes how hard the story leans on you.

peaceful

Damage off, creatures off. Walk the whole maze as a place, not a threat.

comfort

Flicker down, grain off, head-bob off, sensitivity to taste. The dread is optional; the record is not.

What is in it, plainly: dark rooms, sudden sounds, a heartbeat that quickens as something closes in, creatures that chase you and can catch you, flickering light, and a real place with a real and painful history. This page loads nothing from anyone else — no ads, no trackers, no analytics.

§ 23 — the web version

The same building, with fewer windows to the outside.

The game also runs in a browser — the same building as the desktop app. Add it as an app from your browser's menu and it opens landscape and full-screen and keeps playing offline. Your solo run is saved in that browser. When the site changes, it picks up the new version the next time you open it (sometimes the time after).

In a browser, fewer things reach outside the game. A wish is posted and read like any other. Photographs develop their caption but are not saved as files, the beacon goes quiet, HOST LAN and JOIN LAN are not offered, and software rendering and locate your body do nothing. On a phone or tablet, see touch.

The site's front page and the browser game at /play/ carry one cookieless counter, GoatCounter, so someone knows whether anyone came: a page view — which page, the site that sent you, your browser and system, your language, screen width and country — with no cookie and no IP address stored, never a name, a wish or the chat. An invite link's room code and pin are taken out of the address before the counter loads, so neither is ever sent. This manual carries none, and neither do the desktop app or the itch.io copy. The standing orders on the site say exactly what it counts.

The case board on the site shares its record with the /recover console in the same browser.

§ 25 — when it goes wrong

Not every stutter is the building.

it freezes
On desktop, turn on software rendering in settings and restart. If the window stops responding, the app waits a few seconds and reloads it, and keeps a log, backrooms.log, in its user-data folder.
it is slow or hot
Set graphics quality to low, or to classic shading, the plainest light. Set a frame-rate limit. Turn off sharp on high-DPI.
testing your device
Add ?gfxbench=1 to the address in a browser for a minute-long benchmark instead of the title screen, or ?gfxstats=1 for a panel of numbers. For when someone asks you how your machine is doing.
the mouse won't look
Click the view. Chat, cards, requests, falling between floors and being laid down (online) all let go of it.
Esc won't close the request
The cursor is in the box. Use cancel, or click outside the box first.
my friends can't hear me
Everyone needs the same release. On PLAY ONLINE, the relay may not have caught up yet; HOST LAN, in the desktop app, works.
my run is gone
SOLO ▶ overwrites the solo save. Online runs are never saved. A save in the browser does not carry into the desktop app, or the other way round.
i'm somewhere else
you woke somewhere slightly else. Something now stands where you saved. You have been moved to the nearest open floor.
the beacon does nothing
It fires only from the desktop app, and only once a target is set. Wait a few seconds between pushes.
the HP bar won't fill
That is not a fault. Each death leaves a scar. A floor you have never set foot on gives some of it back.
