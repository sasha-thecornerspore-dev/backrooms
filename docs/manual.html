FIELD MANUAL · REV. 1.3.4 · SURVIVAL DIFFICULTY: RISING
Orientation

Where you are

An endless office maze under buzzing fluorescent light — damp yellow wallpaper, mottled carpet, and a hum that never stops. It generates forever in every direction and it does not care that you are here.

The only way is down. Four floors lie beneath you, each stranger and less safe than the last, and somewhere in the wallpaper are the thin places you can slip through. Find them, descend, and try to keep your body — and your mind — intact.

This is not a race. It is a place. Learn its rules or become part of it.

Survival · Body & mind

Staying alive, staying sane

❤ Hit points

The things down here hurt you on contact. Take a hit and the screen jolts red. Level 0 is safe — nothing hunts the lobby — but every floor below has teeth.

Health regrows slowly once you're clear of danger. A bandage patches you fast. Reach zero and you black out — and wake a floor above, beside the hole you fell through, lighter: whatever was in your hand is gone. With a friend on your floor you go down instead, and they have a moment to reach you.

Listen: a heartbeat rises and quickens as something closes in. If you can hear your pulse, it's already near.

🧠 Sanity

The dark and the hunt eat at your mind, and so does a thing you cannot stop looking at. Let it run low and the walls close in violet-black, the whispers start, and the floor finds more ways to frighten you.

Keep it up: your flashlight, a sip of almond water, a page you have not read, and — best of all — staying near a friend. You steady each other. Split up and you both fray. (Stand together long enough and even that stops helping, until you have been apart a while.)

Survival · Light

Light is a resource

Press L for your flashlight — it throws a warm cone that steadies your nerves. Turn it off and the deep floors swallow you (sometimes worth it, to not be seen). A cracked glowstick washes the world green and shoves the fog back for a while. The pipes and the electrical station are black; bring your own light.

Light can be shared. Once you have taken your first way down, stand dark inside a friend's light — a few steps from them, nothing between you — and the things have a harder time finding you, and the dark eats at you slower. If not all of you arrived, it gives your mind nothing — the light goes through you. You'll know whose light you're standing in when you switch yours off. It never helps the one carrying it.

The world · Four floors down

The descent

The maze is no longer one endless yellow floor. It is a stack, and each level has its own look, its own things, and its own way down. The exits are glowing beacons — a beam and a breathing ring, carrying through the fog. Walk up to one and press F to fall through. Your compass (top-left, under your bars) points to the nearest way on that you have seen, and how far; before you have seen one, it only says something pulls, faintly. A second line points back the way you came.

LEVEL ∅ · THE BLOCK

The Block

Every solo run begins outside, in the one real place in the game: an inner-block park in Harlem Park, West Baltimore, under an open grey sky. Rowhouse backs of formstone, brick and plywood; front doors sealed with concrete block; black open windows; a light on in a house where nobody comes out. A form lies on the counter with your name already on it. A plan erased the street and left this.

Descend and the world changes around you — palette, fog, what's in it. Your hit points and inventory come with you. The floor you left remembers you: what you took stays taken, and what you set down lies where you left it.

One building

The floors are stacked in one building. Under some holes a stairwell up climbs back to the floor you fell from, a few rooms over from where you landed. Somewhere on the habitable zone a lift stands, uncalled — it goes one place, and it does not come back for you. A machine you emptied clunks again only after you have been away a while.

The map

Press Tab and you start drawing: pencil strokes for what you have walked, small glyphs for what you have seen — ways, machines, pages, souls, the places it hurt you. You can keep walking while you read it, at half pace. The building moves; a hole is not always where you drew it.

Drawers

Cabinets, crates, boxes, drums and panels hold things. Stand by one, press F and hold still — a step away leaves it, and nothing close may be hunting you. The deep floors count the drawers you open. Some of them hold something that was waiting.

Deep finds

On the deepest floors the machines sometimes give stranger things, and you keep them — they cannot be set down or lost: a survey plumb that reads the grain of the floor, ballast that brings the whole of you back, and an extension slip, 30150A, the one line the system never closed.

The world · Field bestiary

What lives down here

Different floors, different things. Learn their silhouettes in the fog — the shape tells you what it wants.

The world · Company

The lost souls

Not everything here wants to hurt you. Rare, pale figures drift the halls — people who fell through and never found the way up.

Walk up to one and press E. They'll speak: lore, warnings, and — if you listen — the occasional real hint about the way down. They know which word your file is under, and sometimes they say so. If the file has stamped you, they can see it — and they will not talk.

“if the lights go out, stop moving. it hunts movement.”

Supplies · What you'll find

Things left lying around

The Backrooms restocks itself. Walk close to an item and press F to take it; Q uses the selected one; X sets it down. Six slots — choose what's worth carrying.

Leave a word

When you set something down, a card asks whether to leave a word with it: six phrases lifted from the pages, m.'s and the replies left beside them. At first it is the same six for that floor and that kind of thing, whoever holds it; once the pages you have read hold six of the twelve phrases, the card offers only words from those. take the left. the dark drinks you back. i am close behind. The more of the record you find, the more of it you can say. Press a number (or 0 for nothing) and the thing becomes a cache, with an arrow for the way you were facing.

Whoever picks it up reads it on the card — the phrase, the arrow turned to where they stand, and who left it. The prompt warns you first: f · take the bandage · left by maddie. The floor keeps your caches between visits. Online the room sees them as they land, and the relay keeps the last of them for whoever comes through after you — come back to a room and your own words are still yours. A hand needs a moment between two words: set a second down in the same breath and it stays in your hands (your hands are not ready.). Picked up, a cache is already yours; the card only folds away. No free text — only what is written on the pages.

The record · Your file

The file has you now

Somewhere above the drop ceiling there is an office, and it keeps a file on everyone who falls through. You never fill it in. It fills itself in — and the Backrooms reads it.

Until your first way down, nothing is written. On the block the intake form lies on the counter (E to read it, or type /intake in the console anywhere). Then you take a way down, and a few breaths after the level's name a line arrives: the file has you now. The column is written once a run, by how you came in.

The column — nobody chooses one

Tenant

the file has you at an address. You came alone, with a name. The street does not mind you; the deep frays you faster, and below the first floor the things find you more easily. Your hands reach a way from further off, your map marks every way the floor has loaded — and on the block, the sealed doors tell you what the file says lives behind them.

Anchored

the file has your body at a pin. You came through a real place, and the body stayed there. Drift too far and it pulls at your mind — the settings panel shows the leash under locate your body. The first photograph on a floor quiets it for a while.

Unnamed

the file cannot spell you. You left the name blank. The things cannot keep hold of you, and a page or a friend steadies you more — but the film will not develop a letter that is yours. Tell a presence call me … and it asks how that is spelled.

Processed

the file opened a line on you. You were the first into a shared room. The souls see the stamp. The radio reads every group to you, and once, the key. Your wishes are amendments, and a floor you fire the beacon on is filed for good.

And over any column, a layer: thin. Drop into a room someone already walks, or come back up from the dark, and not all of you arrived. The things lose you faster and hear you less — but the light goes through you, and your ward pushes you back too. Ballast cures it.

Filed under — the word you choose

The file also keeps a status. It starts as a notice nobody answered. Once the file can hear you — the numbers station has read you its last group, you have found five of m.'s pages, or you have gone deep enough — the request at a presence shows three faint stamps. Tap one, or type it. A file that already holds something — a closing reached, pages given up — asks on the card first, because a new word lets go of all of it. The office files once a day. Your status is not part of any save: it follows you into every run, and into every room.

Extension

let it stay open. The deep holds you; the street is the thin place. Sour water stops taking anything from you — somewhere a line moves instead.

Compliance

close the file. The film turns honest. A page you have not read comes sealed: read it (E), or leave it unread (X) and give it to the file.

Litigation

contest it. The claim develops on every photograph, even on the darkest floors. A thin figure caught on film is evidence.

The lost souls know which word you are under, and so does the room: — maddie entered the level, filed under extension.

Three ways a file closes

The stand

Under extension, on the deepest floor, once the station has read you its last group: stand in the dark. Light off, still, nothing near you, your mind still your own. While you hold it the dark does not eat you, and the lights steady to tell you it is counting. Long enough, and the notice is extended over you. twenty-two years.

Compliance

Give thirteen pages up unread, then ask a presence to close the file. Each page you leave tells you how many the file has. Under compliance the middle stamp at a presence is close the file, with the count beside it — tap it once the count reaches thirteen. The shimmer leaves the walls. The floors stop leaving pages out. There is no one left to ask.

The seam

Litigation's ending is the oldest one. The station in the deep is counting toward something, and the extension slip names the one line the system never closed. Claim it at a presence; push it into the dark with your beacon. Under litigation, the held seam closes your file. Under the notice nobody answered, nobody writes it down — and it can be held again, any run.

Reading your file

Open settings ⚙ during a run: your file shows the word you are under and since when, how the file has you, and each step of your closing, struck · done as you meet it. Or press Enter and type /status. Request a new notice puts you back under the notice nobody answered — the old file stays closed. If it holds a closing or pages given up, the button asks once before it lets them go.

The floors keep count

A wish sent from a filed run carries its word — filed under: EXTENSION · level 2 — and every release recounts them. A floor whose files lean one way leans with them: the machines stock for it and stamp the tray, the pages and the souls come more or less often, and on the near floors the radio reads the floor's roll call. Who is standing there with you moves it too.

Together · Online co-op

Play with someone

Get into the same world

Type your name on the title screen, hit PLAY ONLINE, pick a room code, and share it. Anyone who enters the same code falls into the same maze — anywhere on the internet, no host and no setup. The first one in decides the world; everyone else inherits it.

See each other

Fellow players appear as pale figures with nameplates in the fog — and a little health bar under each name so you know when to help.

Chat

Press Enter to talk. Messages float as speech bubbles over your head, you'll see when someone's typing…, and /me emotes.

Stay close

A friend nearby steadies your sanity. The halls are less maddening together — so don't wander off alone into the dark.

Call out

Press C (or CALL on a phone) to whistle — two notes at a pitch that is only yours. Everyone on your floor hears it from where you stand, with a line in their chat: near ↗, far ←. The hall counts who has answered lately and tells you in words — three of you. A friend beside you who has not whistled back is not counted yet: nobody has answered yet. A friend who goes ninety seconds without a whistle, a word or standing near you has gone quiet, and it weighs on you. Alone, the hall keeps the sound. Usually. The things hear every call.

Down, not dead

With a friend on your floor, a hit that would kill you lays you down instead: your light goes out and you have 25 seconds. You can still whistle. Find a friend who is down, face them and press F to stay with them — your light on, holding still while you count them back. A step, Space or Esc lets go. If nobody comes, it is a death — and a friend who is down too cannot come: with everyone on the floor lying down, the hit kills you as it always did.

Share the light

Stand dark in a friend's flashlight — a few steps away, nothing between you — and the things lose you more easily while the dark eats at you slower. If not all of you arrived, it gives your mind nothing — the light goes through you. Switch yours off and you'll hear whose light it is: you stand in maddie's light.

Push for each other

The things are not the same things on every screen. When a friend wards, it lands on yours too, from where they stand — and if you were in front of the push when it met something, it steadies you. maddie pushes the dark off you.

Evidence

A friend's photograph of you is proof you exist. For ninety seconds your mind will not sink below a quarter, and the others see you solid, however thin you've worn. Photograph a friend who is down and they're counted back at once.

Update together. The whistle, the kneel, a word left with a thing, a friend's light, ward and photograph pass only between copies of the same release; an older copy still walks beside you, but hears none of them. On PLAY ONLINE they ride the public relay, so a room on a relay that has not been updated plays as it always did — walking, chat and nameplates. HOST LAN from this release carries them from the start.

Meta · Keeping your run

Save, settings & anchors

Continue where you left off

Solo runs auto-save — your level, position, health and whole inventory, what every floor remembers of you, who the file has you as, and your map — every few seconds and on every descent. CONTINUE on the title screen drops you right back in. The word you are filed under is not in the save: it follows you into every run.

Make it yours

The gear ⚙ opens a control panel: music & volume, ambience & volume, film grain, particles, crosshair, head-bob, mouse sensitivity — and, if you want it gentler, turn creatures off, switch to peaceful (no damage), or let solid furniture go and walk through things. For your machine: graphics quality (auto adapts; classic shading is the plainest), reduce flicker for softer light dips and a gentler flash, a frame-rate limit, sharp high-DPI, and on desktop auto-update and software rendering. The beacon's target lives here too, and during a run, your file.

Anchors — fall through a real place

On the title screen you can paste a Google Maps link (or bare lat,lng) into the anchor field. You'll fall through that spot — and the same place always makes the same maze, for everyone. Settings can even reopen the map where you fell in. Join someone else's room and your pin stays yours: the others are told only a mark — enough to know whether you came through the same place they did.

Anchored worlds hold their shape. Unanchored ones forget you were ever there.
