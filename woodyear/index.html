WOODYEAR STREET

struck from the block. kept on the file. you found it anyway.
◂ back to the finding

You decoded the cross-reference and typed a name that has not had a door on the block since the 1961 plan came for it. There is a page here anyway. There should not be. That is the whole point of it.

Woodyear Street ran inside the block — one of the narrow interior lanes threaded behind the main-street rowhouses of Harlem Park, lined with small alley houses where families lived four and five rooms deep into the square. When the 1961 renewal plan came for the interiors, Woodyear's houses came down, and hundreds of people on Woodyear, Vincent and the other back alleys were relocated. The lane was cleared, the ground opened into one of the fenced inner-block greens the residents never asked to keep, and nothing on that stretch has carried an address since. Woodyear still runs north and south of Harlem Park. Through the block, it does not.

And here is what the plan could not reach. A place can be un-built; a name, once filed, is stubborn. Woodyear kept its name — on the city's own parcel file, two hundred-odd doors north and south of here, and in the 1961 plan that cleared it. The block was made to forget a street ever ran through it. The record never got the memo. That is why you could type it and arrive.

A thing can lose its place and keep its name. On the file, the erased do not disappear. They are only moved to a column no one reads on purpose.

The plan cleared the alley houses on two interior streets. This is the recoverer's note on the other one — filed, as ever, sideways.

  1. Very little of it stands inside its block now. Past the fence, it runs on.
  2. Its alley houses came down in the sixties, with Woodyear's, and hundreds of people were moved.
  3. No plaque marks it; a city does not commemorate a subtraction.
  4. Counted still in the city's parcel file, two doors deep.
  5. Even here, two doors still answer to its name. The rest of it is grass.
  6. Nothing runs where most of it ran but a fenced green no one asked to keep.
  7. The other cleared street kept its name on file too — read the first letter of each line, and go to it.
