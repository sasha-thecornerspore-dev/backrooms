WOODYEAR STREET

struck from the map. kept on the file. you found it anyway.

You decoded the cross-reference and typed a name that has not been a place since 1961. There is a page here anyway. There should not be. That is the whole point of it.

Woodyear Street ran inside the block — one of the narrow interior lanes threaded behind the main-street rowhouses of Harlem Park, lined with small alley houses where families lived four and five rooms deep into the square. When the 1961 renewal plan came for the interiors, Woodyear's houses came down among the first, and most of the people in them learned where they were going to live after the decision had already been filed. The lane was cleared, the ground opened into one of the fenced inner-block greens the residents never asked to keep, and the street itself was quietly lifted out of the survey — de-mapped, in the language of the file.

And here is what the plan could not reach. A place can be un-built; a name, once filed, is stubborn. Woodyear kept its name in the deed books, in the old sewer schematics that still route beneath the green, in the relocation notices that outlived the houses they emptied. The city agreed to forget it was ever a street. The record never got the memo. That is why you could type it and arrive.

A thing can lose its place and keep its name. On the file, the erased do not disappear. They are only moved to a column no one reads on purpose.

The plan struck two interior streets. This is the recoverer's note on the other one — filed, as ever, sideways.

  1. Vanished from the survey in the same spring, with the rest of the block's inside.
  2. Its alley houses came down before most residents were told where they would be sent.
  3. No plaque marks it; a city does not commemorate a subtraction.
  4. Cross-referenced now only in deed books, sewer maps, and the notices that outlived it.
  5. Every map drawn since has agreed, quietly, to forget it was a street.
  6. Nothing runs where it ran but a fenced green the block never asked to keep.
  7. The other struck street kept its name on file too — read the first letter of each line, and go to it.
